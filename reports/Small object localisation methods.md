# Sharpen the box loss before adding modules

The cheapest likely route to higher AP75 for this detector is to change how the existing RoI head is trained and how it scores and post-processes boxes. Three things look better than new feature modules: a stronger high-IoU regression signal without raising the overall L1 weight (alpha-GIoU, Dynamic SmoothL1, KL-loss variance), localisation-aware ranking (k3, or RS loss later), and post-processing that costs nothing (score threshold, Soft-NMS). The model's AP75/AP50 ratio is **0.67**. That is close to a plain ResNet-50 Faster R-CNN on SARDet-100K (0.65) and well below strong SAR detectors (0.72-0.80), so localisation precision is the bottleneck, not recall. The codebase changes the picture in two ways. First, the anchors already start at **8 px on P2**, so most of the label-assignment gains reported on AI-TOD (where baselines start at 32 px anchors) do not apply here. Second, the box loss is divided by the number of *sampled* RoIs, so the pending k2 run (256 instead of 512 RoIs) also roughly doubles the per-positive box-loss weight. That makes k2 a partial copy of k5, not a clean test of sampling. Most published evidence comes from ResNet-FPN on COCO or AI-TOD, so expect gains here of about 0.3-1.5 mAP per change (about 0.6-3 dm), not the +2-10 AP the papers report. The ranked list at the end puts the zero-parameter, few-line changes first, because the earlier negatives share a pattern: anything that adds spatial modules on frozen features, raises IoU bars (and so starves the head of positives), or composites unrealistic scenes has failed.

## The gap is box tightness, and the earlier negatives say why

On SARDet-100K, the same Faster R-CNN gains about **+7 AP75 and +6 AP_S** from a stronger backbone (R50 to ConvNeXt-B with MSFA: 54.7 to 61.5 AP75). SARATR-X-v2's lead over previous methods grows "as the localization criterion tightens": +7.3 AP75 against +2.2 AP50 ([SARDet-100K](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06534v3); [SARATR-X-v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23238)). This model's ratio (0.490/0.728 = 0.67) sits on the weak end of that range, while AP_small/mAP (0.88) is in line with SARDet-100K entries. So small objects are not disproportionately bad. Boxes are just loose at every size, and small boxes suffer most because a 2 px error costs a 16 px box far more IoU than a 64 px one.

The negatives fall into three groups, and each rules out a family of next steps. **Raising IoU bars failed**: Cascade R-CNN and RPN fg IoU 0.6. With a median of 2 GTs per image, higher thresholds leave the head with very few positives. On SARDet-100K, Cascade R-CNN with a trainable CNN backbone does give +2.9 to +5.1 AP75 ([SARDet-100K](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06534v3)), so the failure here is specific to the setup, not to cascades in general. Dynamic R-CNN's adaptive IoU threshold, Cascade RPN (which needs an R-CNN IoU of 0.65 for its gain) and SABL-plus-cascade all carry the same risk. **Spatial and feature modules on frozen DINOv3 failed**: ViT-Adapter, block fusion, Convpass variants and the WST stem. That lowers the prior for ViT-CoMer-, DEIMv2-STA- or EFPN-style branches, even though they report +4-6 AP on COCO with full fine-tuning ([ViT-CoMer](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.07392v3); [DEIMv2](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.20787v3)). **Compositing failed**: mosaic and k1. The test images have one class and about two objects, and Ship-Go argues that copy-paste, CutMix and mosaic shift SAR training data away from the real distribution ([Ship-Go](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0924271623003350)).

The code explains k1's damage concretely. `copy_paste` in `sarbench/data.py` rejects a paste only if the *pasted box's* IoU with an existing GT exceeds 0.3. It then overwrites the whole crop rectangle, including the margin. A 10 px GT under a 40 px paste has IoU of about 0.06, so the paste is accepted, the GT's pixels are erased, and its label survives as a phantom target for both the classifier and the GIoU term. Any neighbouring objects inside the donor crop arrive unlabeled. Simple Copy-Paste removes fully occluded objects and trims partially occluded boxes ([Copy-Paste](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2012.07177)); this implementation does neither. j1 still pastes at p=0.5 with up to 3 objects, so this corruption sits underneath *every* pending k ablation, and k6 is the most important of them to read first.

Label assignment is less promising here than the literature suggests. RFLA takes Faster R-CNN on AI-TOD from 11.1 to 21.1 AP, and SimD to 23.9 AP and 17.6 AP75 (from 7.6). Almost all of that is on objects under 16 px, where 32 px default anchors have IoU near 0. On 16-32 px objects the gain is only +2.8 to +4.7 AP_s, and AP_m can fall ([RFLA](https://arxiv.org/html/2208.08738v2); [SimD](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2407.02394)). This model's `AnchorGenerator` already uses sizes (8, 11) on P2, and the max(IoU, NWD) trial was negative. A top-k ranking assigner remains technically untested, because h3 thresholded a new metric rather than ranking. Its expected value is still low.

## The regression loss has more headroom than the weight knob

Published two-stage results agree that the IoU-loss family helps AP75 a little, but the details matter for small objects. On Faster R-CNN R50-FPN, GIoU instead of Smooth L1 gives about +0.9 AP and +0.8 to +1.2 AP75. CIoU gives the best AP75 (41.96) but the *lowest* AP_S (21.32 vs 21.66 for DIoU), because its aspect-ratio term dilutes the centre-distance term that dominates small-box error ([GIoU](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1902.09630); [DIoU](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.08287)). **Alpha-IoU with alpha=3** is the best-supported cheap upgrade. In one MMDetection Faster R-CNN setup, GIoU to alpha-GIoU moves AP from 37.62 to 39.18, AP75 from 41.00 to 42.00, AP_S from 21.49 to 22.05, and the AP75:95 band from 22.43 to 23.46 ([Alpha-IoU](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2110.13675)). Alpha-DIoU is marginally higher. In `sarbench/losses.py` this means replacing `1 - giou` with a power-alpha IoU plus the GIoU or DIoU penalty: a few lines, zero parameters.

Libra R-CNN gives the critical warning for the pending weight ablations. Scaling the Smooth L1 weight raises AP75 but steadily cuts AP_S. Going from 1.0x to 1.5x gives 38.4 to 39.7 AP75 but 21.2 to 20.8 AP_S; 2.0x gives 39.5 AP75 and **20.2 AP_S**. Plain L1 at weight 2.0 drops AP_S to 20.1 ([Libra R-CNN](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.02701)). The authors blame outlier gradients from hard samples. Balanced L1 fixes this (37.2 AP, 40.0 AP75, 21.3 AP_S) by boosting inlier gradients instead of scaling everything. Two caveats apply. This project's loss is L1 *plus* GIoU, which no paper ablates. And published IoU-loss setups replaced L1 entirely at weights of about 10-12, on a different delta scale (torchvision encodes with weights 10,10,5,5). The direction is still clear: scaling the IoU term is safer than scaling the L1 term, and Libra's harmful 2.0x setting matches the current `loss_box_reg` of 2.0, so 4.0 extrapolates past it.

Two zero-parameter or near-zero-parameter options target high IoU directly. **Dynamic SmoothL1**, which shrinks beta from statistics of the regression error, alone gives +1.0 AP and +3.5 AP90 on COCO. Its sibling, dynamic IoU thresholds, is the risky half and should be skipped here ([Dynamic R-CNN](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2004.06002)). The code already uses beta = 1/9 and adds GIoU, which blunts beta's effect, so expect less than the paper. **KL loss with variance voting** adds 4 x 10 log-variance outputs (about 41 k parameters on the 1024-wide head) and a post-NMS merge weighted by predicted uncertainty. Its gain concentrates at AP80-AP90 (Mask R-CNN AP90 11.3 to 17.0). On R50-FPN Faster R-CNN, though, AP75 rises only from 41.1 to 41.6 and AP_S *falls* from 21.5 to 21.0 before Soft-NMS is added. The VGG16 detector gains far more (AP75 22.8 to 30.4), so the benefit shrinks as the base gets stronger ([KL loss](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.08545)). That puts KL loss behind alpha-IoU for this metric.

Heavier head swaps have better COCO evidence but do not fit the remaining budget, which is about **1.1 M trainable** (38.86 of 40 M used). SABL gives the largest AP75 jump (39.3 to 42.8; AP90 8.3 to 18.8) for about +1.2 M parameters ([SABL](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1912.04260)). Double-Head gives +3.2 AP75 and +1.5 AP_S, but its conv branch has 7.19 M parameters ([Double-Head](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1904.06493)). Either one requires shrinking the 1024-wide 2fc head, which d3 and d4 already explored as a reallocation. Grid R-CNN's 56 x 56 heatmaps suit very small RoIs poorly, and enlarging proposals cut its AP_S from 22.1 to 20.8 ([Grid R-CNN](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1811.12030)).

## Ranking and post-processing decide how much localisation shows up in AP

The k3 design (score x predicted IoU^0.5) has a documented trade-off. IoU-Net's IoU-guided NMS raises AP80 from 31.2 to 33.9 and AP90 from 9.8 to 13.3, but **AP50 falls from 58.0 to 56.0**, for a net +0.9 AP ([IoU-Net](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1807.11590)). With dm driven by mAP (0.50:0.95), a net mAP gain still pays even if AP50 drops. But the right comparison is mAP at the best alpha from the planned 0-2 sweep, and alpha=0 isolates the auxiliary-loss effect from the re-ranking. The strongest verified alternative trains the ranking into the classifier instead of fusing it at test time. **RS loss** reaches 39.6 AP and 43.0 AP75 against 37.6 and 41.0 for GIoU on Faster R-CNN R50-FPN. It uses sigmoid classifiers, GIoU weighted by score, auto-balanced task weights, and *no samplers*: the RoI head trains on the top 1000 proposals, and removing the samplers alone is worth about +1 AP ([RS Loss](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2107.11669)). That is high effort here, since it needs a custom loss and a sigmoid head, and it points in the opposite direction from k2's smaller RoI batch.

Post-processing is free and should be checked before any retraining. Soft-NMS lifts R50-FPN Faster R-CNN from 37.9 to 38.6 AP, **41.1 to 42.4 AP75** and 21.5 to 21.9 AP_S without retraining ([KL loss](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1809.08545); [Soft-NMS](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_ICCV_2017/papers/Bodla_Soft-NMS_--_Improving_ICCV_2017_paper.pdf)). With about two objects per image and few overlaps, expect a fraction of that. On the other side, single-model WBF is *worse* than NMS ([WBF](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1910.13302)), and DIoU-NMS adds only +0.1 AP75 ([DIoU](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1911.08287)). `sweep_postprocess.py` already sweeps score threshold (down to 0.001), NMS threshold and detections per image. Running it on j1 and on every k checkpoint, and adding a Gaussian Soft-NMS option (about 40 lines replacing `batched_nms`), makes each ablation's comparison fair and may be worth a few tenths of mAP on its own.

## Feature-side and SAR-specific levers are second-tier under these constraints

A few feature changes fit in about 1 M parameters and have small-object evidence. **Content-aware upsamplers** in CNN-FPN Faster R-CNN raise AP_S from 21.3 with nearest-neighbour to 22.9-23.3 (CARAFE 23.3, DySample 22.9), for tens of thousands of parameters ([DySample](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.15085)). Swapped into the SimpleFeaturePyramid's stride-16 to 8 to 4 path, they directly feed the P2 features that about 95% of RoIs depend on. Two caveats: ViTDet already uses learned transposed convolutions rather than nearest-neighbour, and spatial modules on frozen features have failed here before. **DenoDet V2**'s band-partitioned frequency block adds +0.33 M parameters and lifts a GFL detector on SARDet-100K from 58.87 to 61.55 AP75 and 49.44 to 51.45 AP_S. But it collapses to 52.5 mAP without the band partition, and it was measured with a trainable CNN backbone ([DenoDet V2](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09392)). Since BM3D already denoises the input, this is speculative. **Input channels** are a zero-parameter lever. In MSFA's ablation, Canny-type edges barely moved AP75 (55.0 vs 54.8), while HOG + Haar + WST reached 55.9 AP75 and 45.7 AP_S ([SARDet-100K](https://arxiv.org/html/2403.06534v3)). Swapping Sobel for a HOG magnitude map is a reasonable one-change test, worth at most about 1 point.

**CFINet's size-dependent RPN threshold**, T_a = max(0.25, 0.20 + 0.15 log(sqrt(wh)/12)), lowers the positive bar for small GTs. The previous RPN change *raised* it, so this tests the other direction. The full CRPN adds +1.4 AP on SODA-D, but the dynamic rule itself beats fixed thresholds by only 0.2-0.4 AP. Lowering RPN positive IoU from 0.7 to 0.5 barely changes recall (AR_eS 24.0 to 24.2) ([CFINet](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.09534)). Proposal recall is probably not the binding constraint. **Localisation distillation** gives +1.8 AP75 at zero inference cost and allows an uncapped teacher ([LD](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.12252v4); [LD summary](https://liner.com/review/localization-distillation-for-dense-object-detection)). But it assumes a distributional box head, which torchvision's delta regressor lacks, and it needs a separate teacher run, so it belongs on a longer horizon. The configs show patch-8 re-tokenisation (h2), an earlier IoU branch (h4) and a P2 heatmap (h6) were already run on f5. Their outcomes are not in the research inputs and should be checked before re-proposing any variant of them.

## How to read the pending k ablations

**k6 (copy-paste off) should be read first and can re-base everything.** If it beats j1, the same-class p=0.5 copy-paste has been costing all along, and every other k delta was measured on a corrupted parent. In that case, adopt k6 as the parent before running the list below. A repaired copy-paste (below) is the follow-up, not a reason to keep the current one.

**k2 (256/0.5 sampling) is confounded.** `fastrcnn_loss` divides both the Smooth L1 and GIoU sums by `labels.numel()`, the number of sampled RoIs. Halving that from 512 to 256 per image roughly **doubles the effective box-loss weight per positive** whenever positives fall short of the 128 cap. With a median of 2 GTs that is the usual case, so the positive-fraction change rarely binds. To read k2, log the mean number of positives per image in j1. If it is well under 128, k2 is mostly "box loss x2 and half the negatives", and its result should be compared with k5 (box loss x2) before crediting sampling. RS loss's evidence that *more* samples (no sampler) help by about 1 AP argues against a pure sampling explanation.

**k5 (loss_box_reg 4.0) is the riskiest pending run.** It scales the L1 term, which Libra shows cuts AP_S at 1.5-2x, and the current 2.0 already matches Libra's harmful setting. Expect AP75 flat or slightly up and AP_small down. Judge it on mAP and AP_small together, not AP75 alone. **k4 (GIoU 2.0)** is the safer version of the same idea, because it adds weight through the IoU term. If k4 helps and k5 does not, that confirms the L1-outlier explanation and favours alpha-GIoU next. **k3** should be judged on mAP at the best alpha from the sweep, accepting an AP50 drop of up to 1-2 points, as IoU-Net predicts. If alpha=0 already beats j1, the gain comes from the auxiliary IoU loss acting as a regulariser, not from re-ranking. Differences under about 0.5 mAP at 30% of training are probably within seed noise; e3 ran a seed repeat, so use its spread as the threshold.

## Conclusion

The literature's big small-object wins, Gaussian assignment and high-resolution feature branches, mostly fix problems this detector does not have: anchors missing tiny boxes, and trainable CNN backbones that can absorb an extra branch. What it does have is a regression signal whose scale depends on the RoI sampling budget, a box-loss weight already at the level where Libra saw AP_S degrade, and a copy-paste that silently erases small GTs. The highest-value work is therefore careful loss engineering and clean comparisons, not architecture. The most useful single diagnostic is to log positives per image, effective box-loss weight and AP_small next to AP75 for every run. That makes k2 and k5 interpretable, and it helps tell a real localisation gain from an AP_small-for-AP75 trade, which dm does not reward.

A strategic point follows. Given the AP75/AP50 gap, SAR-pretrained B-size backbones (SARATR-X family, iTPN-B or HiViT-B, about 90 M parameters) are the one lever with large documented AP75 gains on SAR, but they replace DINOv3 rather than tune around it ([SARATR-X-v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.23238)). If the loss-side list above stalls below about +1.5 mAP total, a frozen-backbone swap trial, after checking for dataset overlap and leakage, is likely worth more than any further head or neck module.
